\textbf{Status: fifth-stage research note.} This is working material, not a
submission-ready CSDA manuscript. All results concern Gaussian models with fixed
covariance-component matrices. Stage one is a float64 single-addition pilot.
Stage two implements complete BIC-type single/pair paths on a compact ratio box.
Stage three verifies exclusion of all unbounded tails under positive projection
residual floors, extending the path guarantee to all nonnegative ratios.
Stage four specializes the verifier to two crossed random-intercept designs
and measures full-path time and process memory under a matched protocol.
Stage five compares five selection procedures on 300 new simulated datasets,
including held-out prediction with new groups on both margins. It is
a new, explicitly defined selection policy, not a reproduction of every published
ARSS-JPS likelihood-ratio rule. These experiments do not establish selective inference.
A focused literature audit identifies substantial prior foundations; no historical
priority claim is made for the individual mathematical ingredients.

\begin{abstract}
The fixed-covariance GLS score used by ARSS-JPS can understate the gain obtained
after re-estimating covariance parameters. We develop computable upper bounds on that
gain for covariance models $V=\sigma^2(I+\sum_{k=1}^q\lambda_kA_k)$ with $A_k\succeq0$.
The key observation is that the logarithm of the profiled GLS residual sum of squares
is convex in the variance ratios, whereas the log determinant is concave. A supporting
plane for the former and vertex values for the latter give a global lower bound on
profile deviance over a box. These bounds permit rigorous candidate exclusion in
exact arithmetic and can share covariance factorizations across candidates. A
positive residual floor additionally permits data-dependent exclusion of unbounded
parameter tails. Complete single-addition and pair-rescue paths under an explicit
BIC-type rule are checked using exact rational rank calculations and Arb
enclosures. In a full-orthant pilot, all 18 paths completed and replayed; two
large-variance examples selected different terminal supports from the $[0,4]^2$
policy. A structured verifier for two crossed random-intercept components combines
smaller Schur solves, shared gradient products and bounded large-object caches.
In a matched scaling pilot with $n=800$, up to 200 random-effect columns or 60
candidates, both backends completed \ScalingComplete{} input cases with identical
decision witnesses. Median full-path speedup was \ScalingRatio{}, and median
process peak-RSS reduction was \ScalingMemoryReduction{}\%.
A subsequent 300-dataset study compares the BIC policy with and without pairs,
the archived ARSS-JPS search rule, and cross-validated elastic-net screening
followed by an ML refit. It measures selection and prediction in entirely new
groups, with explicit completion rates and Monte Carlo uncertainty.
These experiments are not universal complexity or performance guarantees. The certificate
establishes the stated computational decisions for the binary input data, not
best-subset optimality or statistical error control.
\end{abstract}

\section{The computational question}
The supplied ARSS-JPS article~\cite{arss} prioritizes candidate fixed effects using a
GLS score at the current covariance estimate and fully refits a shortlist. Its
fixed-covariance identity does not certify the ranking after variance re-estimation.
The present task is to identify the best addition over a specified candidate family
without running a separate full optimizer for every candidate, and to return an
explicit unresolved result if separation cannot be established within the budget.

\begin{samepage}
There are three different statements to distinguish:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a numerical local optimizer found a feasible likelihood value;
\item a valid bound proves that a rival cannot beat that feasible value;
\item the selected variables are true effects or their reported $p$-values are calibrated.
\end{enumerate}
\end{samepage}
The theory addresses the first two; stage five measures statistical performance
empirically. A selected best addition under the complete
null is still a best addition; it need not be accepted by a subsequent stopping rule.

\section{Model, profile objective and the score discrepancy}
Let $X_M$ be the current fixed-effect design and let $X_C$ contain a prespecified
candidate block. Write $X_{M+C}=[X_M,X_C]$. For each tested design assume full column
rank and $y\notin\Span(X_{M+C})$. Consider
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model}
y\sim N(X_{M+C}\beta,\sigma^2R(\lambda)),\qquad
R(\lambda)=I_n+\sum_{k=1}^q\lambda_kA_k,
\quad A_k\succeq0,\quad \lambda_k\geq0.
\end{equation}
The matrices $A_k$ are fixed. They need not commute; for independent crossed
intercepts, $A_k=Z_kZ_k^\top$. The residual scale is unrestricted and positive.

For any tested design $X$, define
\begin{align}
P_X(R)&=R^{-1}-R^{-1}X(X^\top R^{-1}X)^{-1}X^\top R^{-1},\\
S_X(\lambda)&=y^\top P_X(R(\lambda))y>0,\\
F_X(\lambda)&=\log\det R(\lambda)+n\log\{S_X(\lambda)/n\}.
\end{align}
After maximizing over $\beta$ and $\sigma^2$, $F_X$ is $-2$ times the log likelihood
up to the common constant $n\{1+\log(2\pi)\}$. This is ML, not REML.
Let $g=\log\det R$ and $h_X=n\log(S_X/n)$, so $F_X=g+h_X$.
This profiling and low-dimensional shape optimization are established;
de Carvalho et al.~\cite{carvalho} derive the corresponding profiled objective
and a compact angular parametrization. They are not claimed as new here.

Fix a covariance-ratio domain $\Theta$ and define $F_X^*=\min_{\Theta}F_X$. If the
current ratio $\widehat\lambda_M$ is a global minimizer on that domain, the full
likelihood-ratio improvement is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lr}
D_C=F_M^*-F_{M+C}^*.
\end{equation}
At $\widehat\lambda_M$, let $d_C=S_M-S_{M+C}$ and
$\widehat\sigma_M^2=S_M/n$. The full-covariance GLS score is
\begin{equation}
Q_C=d_C/\widehat\sigma_M^2=nd_C/S_M.
\end{equation}
For a scalar addition, $d_C=(x_C^\top P_My)^2/(x_C^\top P_Mx_C)$; for a block the
usual residualized Gram inverse gives the analogous expression.
Merely profiling the scale at the same ratios already yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scale}
D_C\ \geq\ n\log\frac{S_M}{S_M-d_C}\ \geq\ Q_C,
\end{equation}
where the second inequality follows from $-\log(1-u)\geq u$ for $0\leq u<1$.
Thus a small $Q_C$ is not an upper bound on what a full refit could achieve.

\textbf{If the current fit is only local.} At any feasible $\lambda_0$, the same
inequality holds with $D_C$ replaced by
$\widetilde D_C=F_M(\lambda_0)-F_{M+C}^*$. It must not be re-labelled as a lower bound
on the globally profiled statistic~\eqref{eq:lr}. Candidate ranking remains
unaffected because the baseline is a common additive constant. The numerical
score checks in this pilot use this explicitly feasible-baseline comparison.

\section{A convex part of the profiled Gaussian objective}
The following ingredients are elementary matrix-convexity facts; we include full
proofs rather than treating them as new statistical phenomena. General background
on supporting planes and log-determinant concavity is in~\cite{boyd}.

\begin{lemma}[Log-convexity of the profiled residual]\label{lem:convex}
For a fixed full-rank design $X$ and $y\notin\Span(X)$, the function
$\lambda\mapsto\log S_X(\lambda)$ is convex on every convex domain on which
$R(\lambda)$ is positive definite. For $A_k\succeq0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gradient}
\partial_k h_X(\lambda)
=-n\,\frac{y^\top P_X A_kP_Xy}{S_X(\lambda)}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $K$ have orthonormal columns spanning $\ker X^\top$. Then
$P_X=K(K^\top RK)^{-1}K^\top$. Along a line $R(t)=R+tA$ in the positive-definite
domain, differentiation gives $P'=-PAP$ and $P''=2PAPAP$. Hence
\[
S'=-y^\top PAPy,\qquad S''=2y^\top PAPAPy.
\]
At the point under consideration set $u=P^{1/2}y$ and $B=P^{1/2}AP^{1/2}$. Since
$S=u^\top u>0$ and $B$ is symmetric, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\begin{align*}
(\log S)''
&=\frac{2u^\top B^2u}{u^\top u}
 -\frac{(u^\top Bu)^2}{(u^\top u)^2}\\
&\geq\frac{(u^\top Bu)^2}{(u^\top u)^2}\geq0.
\end{align*}
This proves convexity on every line. Taking $A=A_k$ yields~\eqref{eq:gradient}.
The directional argument does not require $A$ to be positive semidefinite.
\end{proof}

This lemma does \emph{not} make the complete profile objective convex: $g$ is
concave. No assumption of global likelihood concavity or uniqueness of a local
optimizer is used below.

\section{A box bound and an explicit correction \texorpdfstring{$B_C$}{B-C}}
Let $B=[a,b]\subset\R_+^q$ be a closed box, and let $\vertx(B)$ denote its $2^q$
vertices. For any anchor $c$ in the covariance domain define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:box}
L_X(B;c)=\min_{v\in\vertx(B)}
\left[g(v)+h_X(c)+\nabla h_X(c)^\top(v-c)\right].
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[A global bound on a box]\label{thm:box}
For every $\lambda\in B$, $F_X(\lambda)\geq L_X(B;c)$. Consequently, for a finite
box partition $\calP$ of $\Theta$ and any finite sets of anchors $\mathcal A_B$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partition}
\underline F_X(\calP)=\min_{B\in\calP}\ \max_{c\in\mathcal A_B}L_X(B;c)
\ \leq\ F_X^*.
\end{equation}
Valid parent-box lower bounds may also be retained in the maximum when a box is split.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Convexity yields $h_X(\lambda)\geq h_X(c)+\nabla h_X(c)^\top(\lambda-c)$.
Express $\lambda=\sum_v w_vv$ as a convex combination of box vertices. Since
$R$ is affine and $\log\det$ is concave,
$g(\lambda)\geq\sum_vw_vg(v)$. Combining the inequalities gives
\[
F_X(\lambda)\geq\sum_vw_v\left[g(v)+h_X(c)+\nabla h_X(c)^\top(v-c)\right]
\geq L_X(B;c).
\]
Taking maxima of valid lower bounds on a box and then the minimum across a covering
partition proves~\eqref{eq:partition}. A parent bound remains valid on each child.
\end{proof}

For comparison, Loewner monotonicity alone gives the simpler bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loewner}
F_X(\lambda)\geq g(a)+n\log\{S_X(b)/n\},\qquad \lambda\in[a,b].
\end{equation}
Indeed, $R(a)\preceq R(\lambda)\preceq R(b)$; the log determinant is increasing and
the minimum GLS residual is decreasing in this order. This bound is implemented
as an optional ablation. All reported main results use~\eqref{eq:box}.

\begin{corollary}[The requested GLS-to-refit bound]\label{cor:B}
If the baseline in~\eqref{eq:lr} is globally optimized on $\Theta$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BC}
Q_C\leq D_C\leq Q_C+B_C(\calP),\qquad
B_C(\calP)=F_M^*-\underline F_{M+C}(\calP)-Q_C\geq0.
\end{equation}
For a merely feasible baseline, replace $F_M^*$ and $D_C$ by $F_M(\lambda_0)$ and
$\widetilde D_C$, respectively.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The lower bound is~\eqref{eq:scale}. The upper bound follows directly from
$F_{M+C}^*\geq\underline F_{M+C}(\calP)$.
\end{proof}

Unlike a local Taylor approximation with an unverified Hessian condition,
\eqref{eq:BC} bounds all covariance values in the stated domain. The cost and
tightness depend on the partition. Computing an arbitrarily tight bound can be
expensive; the result does not imply a universal speed-up.

\subsection{How the bound tightens}
\begin{theorem}[Quadratic consistency of the box bound]\label{thm:rate}
Suppose $0\preceq\nabla^2 h_X\preceq H_XI$ on $B$, and let $c$ be its midpoint.
If $d_B=\|b-a\|_2$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate}
0\leq\min_{\lambda\in B}F_X(\lambda)-L_X(B;c)
\leq H_Xd_B^2/8.
\end{equation}
On a root box with lower corner $a_0$, one valid, generally conservative choice is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H}
H_X=2n\sum_{k=1}^q\alpha_k^2,\qquad
\alpha_k=\|R(a_0)^{-1/2}A_kR(a_0)^{-1/2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each vertex $v$, smooth convexity gives
$0\leq h_X(v)-h_X(c)-\nabla h_X(c)^\top(v-c)\leq H_X\|v-c\|^2/2
=H_Xd_B^2/8$. Therefore every expression minimized in~\eqref{eq:box} is at least
$\min_BF_X-H_Xd_B^2/8$. The opposite inequality is Theorem~\ref{thm:box}.

To prove~\eqref{eq:H}, in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:convex} use
$(\log S)''\leq2\|P^{1/2}AP^{1/2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2$. Since
$P\preceq R^{-1}$, $R\succeq R(a_0)$ and $A_k\succeq0$,
$\|P^{1/2}A_kP^{1/2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\alpha_k$. For
$A=\sum_kd_kA_k$, the triangle inequality and Cauchy--Schwarz bound the directional
second derivative of $h_X$ by $2n(\sum_k\alpha_k|d_k|)^2
\leq2n\sum_k\alpha_k^2\|d\|^2$.
\end{proof}

If an incumbent has feasible objective $U_*$ and every rival minimum is at least
$U_*+\delta$, a partition with
$\max_Bd_B^2<8\delta/\max_CH_{M+C}$ suffices to exclude every rival. This is a
gap-dependent sufficiency result. It does not prove that a particular best-first
implementation has that many steps, nor that a local incumbent always has such
a gap. An exact tie has no positive separation gap.

\section{Excluding the unbounded variance-ratio tails}
The compact-domain restriction can be removed under an additional, checkable
condition. Suppose $A_k=Z_kZ_k^\top$, let $Z=[Z_1,\ldots,Z_q]$, and define
\begin{equation}
s_X^{\infty}=\dist\{y,\Span(X,Z)\}^2.
\end{equation}
The projection argument has a close antecedent in the coercivity proof of
Zhou et al.~\cite{zhou}, Lemma 3. The formulation below makes candidate-specific
floors and explicit coordinate cutoffs available to the screening calculation.
This is calculated separately for each candidate; it is \emph{not} the residual
from fitting all candidate columns simultaneously.

\begin{theorem}[Data-dependent exclusion of all tails]\label{thm:tail}
For every $\lambda\in\R_+^q$, $S_X(\lambda)\geq s_X^{\infty}$. Assume
$s_*:=\min_{C\in\calC}s_{M+C}^{\infty}>0$ and each $A_k$ has positive rank.
Let $T$ be the objective value of any feasible candidate and let $\eta>0$.
Choose $U_k<\infty$ so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail}
\log\det(I+U_kA_k)+n\log(s_*/n)\geq T+\eta.
\end{equation}
Then every candidate has objective at least $T+\eta$ outside
$\Theta=\prod_k[0,U_k]$. Hence the globally best candidate over all nonnegative
variance ratios can be identified by screening on this finite box.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On the orthogonal complement of $\Span(Z)$, $R$ and $R^{-1}$ act as the identity,
and on $\Span(Z)$ the inverse is positive definite. Thus
$R^{-1}\succeq\Pi_{\Span(Z)^\perp}$. Minimizing over $\beta$ yields
\[
S_X(\lambda)\geq\min_{\beta}\|\Pi_{\Span(Z)^\perp}(y-X\beta)\|^2
=\min_{\beta,u}\|y-X\beta-Zu\|^2=s_X^{\infty}.
\]
If $\lambda_k\geq U_k$, then $R(\lambda)\succeq I+U_kA_k$. Apply determinant
monotonicity and the residual floor to obtain~\eqref{eq:tail} as a lower bound on
$F_{M+C}(\lambda)$. A finite $U_k$ exists because $A_k$ has positive rank and its
log determinant diverges with $U_k$. Every point outside the box violates at least
one coordinate bound. The incumbent is feasible with value $T$, so no outside point
can attain the best value.
\end{proof}

The box need not contain the minimizer of an irrelevant rival whose minimum is
already worse than $T$. For a global bound on that rival's objective, use the
minimum of its inside-box lower bound and its tail lower bound. If the positive
residual-floor condition fails, this tail theorem is unavailable; it is a
sufficient condition, not a necessary condition for existence of ML estimates.

\section{Candidate exclusion, implementation and stopping}
\begin{corollary}[Correct exclusion]\label{cor:exclude}
Let $\overline F_{M+C_*}=F_{M+C_*}(\lambda_*)$ be feasible. If
$\underline F_{M+C}>\overline F_{M+C_*}$, candidate $C$ cannot minimize the profiled
objective among the candidate family. If this strict inequality holds for every
rival, $C_*$ is the unique best candidate. The incumbent need not have been
globally optimized. Fixed, model-specific penalties can be added to both bounds.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
$F_{M+C}^*\geq\underline F_{M+C}>\overline F_{M+C_*}\geq F_{M+C_*}^*$.
\end{proof}

The stage-one, scalar-only prototype performs the following operations.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Fit the current model from a common deterministic set of starts. Evaluate
all one-variable additions at its ratios and locally refit the leading candidate.
\item Construct the root-box lower bounds using its midpoint and the current
model's ratio as two supporting-plane anchors.
\item Exclude every rival whose global partition lower bound exceeds the best
feasible objective, with a numerical cushion in the float64 implementation.
\item Split the box with the smallest remaining rival bound along its longest
side after normalizing by root-box widths. Evaluate all candidate profiles at the
two new midpoints and preserve valid parent bounds.
\item If a midpoint identifies a better feasible candidate, locally refit it and
update the incumbent. Repeat until strict separation or the budget is exhausted.
\end{enumerate}
Its return status is \texttt{separated} or \texttt{unresolved}.
An unresolved incumbent must not be presented as certified. The code does not
silently resolve an exact tie by claiming a strict inequality.

\subsection{Shared covariance calculations}
Let $r=\sum_k\operatorname{col}(Z_k)$ and let $D$ repeat $\lambda_k$ along the
corresponding random-effect columns. With $W=ZD^{1/2}$,
\begin{equation}
R^{-1}=I-W(I+W^\top W)^{-1}W^\top,
\qquad \log\det R=\log\det(I+W^\top W).
\end{equation}
Precompute $Z^\top Z$, $Z^\top[X_M,X_{\calC},y]$, and the required base/candidate
cross products. One $r\times r$ Cholesky factorization at each anchor serves every
candidate. For scalar additions, only candidate diagonal Gram terms and their
cross products with $X_M$ and $y$ are needed. The code does not build an $n\times n$
covariance or a $p\times p$ candidate Gram matrix. Gradient components follow from
$y^\top P_XA_kP_Xy=\|Z_k^\top P_Xy\|^2$.

The number of vertices is $2^q$ and both implementations have dense cubic
cost in $r$. This pilot targets few covariance components and modest random-effect
rank. Large crossed grouping systems require a sparse implementation. Reusing
Woodbury identities is established linear algebra, not by itself a novelty claim.

\subsection{Likelihood-ratio decisions}
For a likelihood-ratio stopping decision, bracket both model minima:
$F_M^*\in[L_M,U_M]$ and $F_{M+C}^*\in[L_C,U_C]$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stop}
D_C\in[L_M-U_C,\ U_M-L_C].
\end{equation}
Accept a specified computational threshold only if the lower endpoint exceeds it;
reject only if the upper endpoint is below it; otherwise refine. Repeating
certified choices and certified threshold decisions yields the corresponding
reference path by induction, subject to a specified tie convention. The new
implementation below uses penalized objective comparisons instead. Exact
ARSS-JPS replication would additionally require its screening gate and joint and
mutual-conditional likelihood-ratio rules. Ordinary repeated tests do not become
valid selective inference merely because their numerical values are
certified~\cite{rugamer}.

\section{A complete single-addition and pair-rescue policy}
The second-stage pilot uses $\Theta=[0,4]^2$; the third-stage pilot uses
$\Theta=\R_+^2$. Both use the criterion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bic}
J(M)=F_M^*+|M|\log n.
\end{equation}
Here $M$ counts the selectable fixed-effect columns, excluding the always-present
base columns. The residual scale and the number of allowed covariance components
are common to every model; they contribute only a common parameter-count term.
We refer to~\eqref{eq:bic} as a BIC-type criterion with observation-count penalty.
Its statistical justification under a particular crossed-effects asymptotic regime
is separate from the computational guarantee established here. Within a stage,
the common term $|M|\log n$ is omitted from every candidate bound.

\textbf{Reference policy.} Starting with the base model, compare the current
support $M$ with all remaining single additions. If the best addition strictly
decreases $J$, accept it and repeat. Otherwise compare $M$ with all remaining
two-variable additions. If the best pair strictly decreases $J$, accept it and
return to the single-addition phase; otherwise stop. The reference retains the
current model on an equal-value stopping tie and uses lexicographic order for
equal improving candidates. Our verified procedure does not force a decision
when the bounds cannot establish strict separation.

Every family includes the current model as candidate zero. Therefore selecting
candidate zero certifies that none of the specified additions improves the
criterion. No residual-correlation gate or top-$L$ truncation is used for pairs.
For $m$ remaining variables there are $\binom{m}{2}$ pairs, all of which are part
of the comparison. This explicit reference distinguishes the procedure from
the published ARSS-JPS protocol.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional correctness of a complete path]\label{thm:path}
Suppose every tested design is full rank with positive residual, and the same
covariance domain and penalty are used at every step. The domain may be compact
or the full orthant covered by the tail certificates below. For a family
$\{M\}\cup\{M+C:C\in\calC\}$, let $L_C\leq J(M+C)$ and let $U_{C_*}$ be
an attained or upward-enclosed feasible penalized value for an incumbent.
If $L_C>U_{C_*}$ for every rival, that family's unique minimizing support is
$M+C_*$. If this property holds at every step of a completed execution, the
execution has the same additions and stopping decisions as the reference policy.
Its terminal support has no improving single or pair addition.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each rival, $J(M+C)\geq L_C>U_{C_*}\geq J(M+C_*)$.
This proves the family decision, including the case $C_*=\varnothing$.
Both procedures start from the same support. Inductively, the same previous
support and phase produce the same complete candidate family and the same
certified next decision. Each accepted block increases support size, so a
completed execution has finitely many additions. At termination the two families
have been rejected, unless there are insufficient remaining variables, in which
case the corresponding family is empty. The claim is about additions, not
deletions, swaps or all possible subsets.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Conditional improvement of a rescued pair]
If single additions have stopped and pair $\{j,k\}$ is accepted, then
$J(M+j+k)<J(M+j)$ and $J(M+j+k)<J(M+k)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Single stopping gives $J(M+j),J(M+k)\geq J(M)$, and pair acceptance gives
$J(M+j+k)<J(M)$. Combine the inequalities.
\end{proof}
Thus both members of a rescued pair satisfy a conditional \emph{BIC-improvement}
condition. This is not the mutual conditional $p$-value criterion from ARSS-JPS.

\section{From analytic bounds to checked numerical decisions}
The new code separates the float64 search from a verifier using Arb, an
arbitrary-precision midpoint-radius interval library~\cite{arb}. The search
proposes an incumbent covariance point and a finite binary partition tree.
Its heuristic numerical cushions do not enter the final proof decision.

\subsection{The numerical contract}
The data arrays $y,X,Z_k$ are interpreted as their exact stored binary values.
All covariance ratios in the witness are also exact binary values. Arb imports
these values and recomputes the data cross products, small-matrix solves,
determinants, logarithms and residual derivatives. It does not import rounded
float64 Gram matrices as exact inputs. The constant $\log n$ in the penalty is
enclosed as well. The contract concerns numerical roundoff, not measurement error
or uncertainty in unrounded source data.

For each anchor $c$ and vertex $v$, the verifier encloses
\begin{equation}
g(v)+h_X(c)+\nabla h_X(c)^\top(v-c)+|C|\log n
\end{equation}
using ball arithmetic. The lower endpoint is a valid lower bound for that
expression. Taking the minimum of these lower endpoints over vertices, the
maximum over anchors and inherited parent bounds, and finally the minimum over
partition leaves preserves validity. At the feasible incumbent point the upper
endpoint of its penalized objective provides $U_{C_*}$. The final comparison
uses verified strict inequalities, not differences of printed decimal midpoints.

\begin{theorem}[Soundness of a checked witness]\label{thm:verified}
Assume the analytic model conditions above and enclosing arithmetic operations.
If the verifier checks a complete partition of $\Theta$, correctly constructs
the lower and upper endpoints just described, and finds every rival lower bound
strictly greater than the incumbent upper bound, its reported family decision
is correct for the stored data. The float64 optimizer need not find a global
minimum, and the float64 search bounds need not themselves be verified.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each enclosed point quantity contains its exact value. The supporting-plane
bound is valid everywhere on its box, and taking a lower endpoint cannot increase
it beyond the exact expression. Minimum, maximum and restriction to a child box
preserve the stated lower-bound properties. Complete leaf coverage yields a
global lower bound on $\Theta$. The incumbent upper endpoint is at least its
attained objective. Apply Corollary~\ref{cor:exclude} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:path}.
\end{proof}

The software validates the input fingerprint, nonnegative feasible covariance
points, the root box, and that every internal node splits into exactly two
children covering its parent. Full-path replay independently constructs all
single/pair families and checks phase order and support changes. Removing a
rival or half of the covariance domain invalidates the witness. Failure to
establish a positive residual, a valid solve or strict separation produces no
certificate. This uses the trusted Arb implementation and the proved formulas;
it is not a machine-checked formal proof of the entire Python program.

\subsection{Implementation and limits}
\texttt{certified\_path.py} implements the block engine, search, verifier and full
policy. Each local fit uses the same deterministic starts as the all-model
comparator: four current-model starts and those four plus the fitted current
ratios for candidates. A new midpoint can supply an additional candidate start.
The search shares covariance factorizations and anchor calculations across the
family. The verifier also caches covariance calculations across path stages.

Unlike the stage-one scalar engine, this block engine forms a full predictor
Gram matrix. Both pair enumeration and the associated storage can grow
quadratically in $p$, and the random-effect solve is dense. This is an implementation
for modest family sizes, not a demonstrated high-dimensional pair algorithm.
Arb uses 128-bit precision in the path pilot; precision can be specified explicitly.
It is not automatically increased until every difficult input resolves.

A checked decision is marked \texttt{verified}; a finished path is
\texttt{complete}. When the next decision cannot be certified, the output is
\texttt{unresolved\_budget} or \texttt{unresolved\_numeric}.
Invalid or rank-deficient input models can raise an explicit error. No unresolved
incumbent is promoted to a certified addition. The original stage-two verifier
retains its compact-box scope. The additional global verifier described next
requires an explicit, independently checked tail certificate for every stage.

\subsection{Verified exclusion of the complement of a box}
Let $d_C=|C|$ and suppose a verified lower bound
$0<\ell_C\leq s_{M+C}^{\infty}$ is available for each support in the family,
including the current model. For cutoffs $U_k>0$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:verifiedtail}
\tau_C=\min_{1\leq k\leq q}\log\det(I+U_kZ_k^\top Z_k)
       +n\log(\ell_C/n)+d_C\log n.
\end{equation}
By determinant monotonicity, Sylvester's determinant identity and
Theorem~\ref{thm:tail}, this is a lower bound on the penalized objective everywhere
outside $B=\prod_k[0,U_k]$. The common $|M|\log n$ term is omitted throughout
this family comparison.

\begin{theorem}[Checked decisions on the nonnegative orthant]\label{thm:global}
Suppose the verifier encloses positive projection floors and the quantities
in~\eqref{eq:verifiedtail}, and verifies a complete partition of $B$. Let
$L_C^{\rm box}$ and $\underline\tau_C$ be the resulting lower bounds, and let
$U_*$ be an upward-enclosed feasible value for a winning support at a point of $B$.
If both $L_C^{\rm box}>U_*$ and $\underline\tau_C>U_*$ hold for every rival,
the winning support is the unique best support in that family over $\R_+^q$.
Completed paths of such decisions follow the full-orthant reference policy.
Different stages may use different certified boxes.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The two bounds cover $B$ and its complement, respectively. Thus
$\min\{L_C^{\rm box},\underline\tau_C\}$ is a lower bound for each rival over
the entire orthant. Strict comparison with the feasible upper bound proves the
decision. The reference domain remains $\R_+^q$ at every stage, even though the
finite cover used to certify it changes. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:path} inductively.
\end{proof}

\textbf{Computing a floor without a numerical rank assumption.}
The matrix $[X_{\rm base},Z]$ is usually rank deficient for crossed intercepts.
\texttt{certified\_global\_path.py} converts each stored binary number to its
exact rational value and uses FLINT rational row reduction to select original
columns that span this entire matrix. This establishes the span, rather than
only verifying independence of a proposed smaller matrix. Arb recomputes the
projection Gram products. After projecting all selectable columns and $y$ off
this base/random space, a small support-specific Schur complement encloses
$s_{M+C}^{\infty}$. If a candidate lies in the already projected space, a full
exact rank reduction of its augmented design supplies a spanning basis instead.
The floor is recomputed from data during replay; saved floor or rank claims are
not accepted as proof. Rational matrix operations are documented in the
python-flint interface cited with~\cite{arb}.

\textbf{Constructing the finite search region.}
Current-model multistart fitting begins on $[0,4]^q$ and enlarges the pilot box
by a factor of four when an upper boundary is active, for at most six expansions.
This supplies only a feasible incumbent. For each coordinate, powers of two
starting at 4 are then tested in Arb until the tail lower bound for every family
member exceeds the incumbent upper bound by 1. These strict inequalities alone
authorize the finite search box. The implementation allows at most 50 doublings.
An unproved positive floor, an identically zero covariance component, or failed
tail construction returns \texttt{unresolved\_tail}; it does not justify a global
decision or establish nonexistence of ML.

Wide cutoffs motivate splitting at the midpoint of $\log(1+\lambda_k)$ and
choosing the coordinate with the largest current logarithmic width. Anchors
use the same geometric center in shifted coordinates. This changes search
geometry only: tangents and all bounds remain in the original affine $\lambda$
coordinates. The verifier checks the resulting exact binary partition endpoints
and does not rely on the search's midpoint formula. Logarithmic subdivision is
a computational refinement, not a claimed new global-optimization principle.

\section{A structured verifier for two crossed intercepts}\label{sec:structured}
The float64 search, BIC policy, variance domain and witness format can remain
unchanged while the verified numerical backend is specialized. Suppose $q=2$
and each row of $Z_k$ contains exactly one entry equal to 1 and all remaining
entries equal to 0. The implementation checks this condition on the original
binary arrays. It does not infer orthogonality by thresholding small products.
For exposition, reorder the components so that $r_L\geq r_S$, and write
\[
Z^\top Z=\begin{pmatrix}N_L&C\\C^\top&N_S\end{pmatrix},\qquad
D_L=I+\lambda_LN_L,\qquad
T=I+\lambda_SN_S-\lambda_L\lambda_SC^\top D_L^{-1}C.
\]
The count matrices $N_L,N_S$ are diagonal. Eliminating the larger diagonal block
leaves a dense Schur complement of dimension $r_S=\min(r_1,r_2)$.

\begin{lemma}[Structured covariance identities]\label{lem:schur}
Let $H$ concatenate all base columns, selectable predictors and $y$, and let
$B_L=Z_L^\top H$, $B_S=Z_S^\top H$. For nonnegative ratios define
\begin{align}
E_S&=T^{-1}(B_S-\lambda_LC^\top D_L^{-1}B_L),\\
E_L&=D_L^{-1}(B_L-\lambda_SC E_S).
\end{align}
Then $E_k=Z_k^\top R^{-1}H$, and
\begin{align}
\log\det R&=\sum_{i=1}^{r_L}\log(1+\lambda_L n_{Li})+\log\det T,\label{eq:schurdet}\\
H^\top R^{-1}H&=H^\top H-\lambda_L B_L^\top E_L-\lambda_S B_S^\top E_S.
\end{align}
These identities also hold when either ratio is zero and when some group columns
are unused. The Schur complement remains positive definite.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $D=\diag(\lambda_LI,\lambda_SI)$, the identity
$Z^\top R^{-1}H=(I+Z^\top ZD)^{-1}Z^\top H$ yields block equations
$D_LE_L+\lambda_SC E_S=B_L$ and
$\lambda_LC^\top E_L+(I+\lambda_SN_S)E_S=B_S$.
Elimination gives the displayed formulas. The determinant follows by block
elimination and Sylvester's identity; the last identity follows from
$R^{-1}=I-ZD(I+Z^\top ZD)^{-1}Z^\top$. For positive ratios, $T$ is the Schur
complement of the positive definite symmetric matrix
$I+D^{1/2}Z^\top ZD^{1/2}$. At either zero ratio its formulas directly reduce
to a positive diagonal matrix. Unused columns contribute identity entries.
\end{proof}

\textbf{Shared gradient products.}
At each anchor compute $G_k=E_k^\top E_k$ once. For a candidate model with full
fixed design $X$ (including its base and current support), let
$v_C$ be the coefficient vector over the columns of $H$ with last entry 1,
fixed-design entries $-\widehat\beta_X$ and other entries zero. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharedgradient}
y^\top P_X A_k P_X y=\|Z_k^\top R^{-1}(y-X\widehat\beta_X)\|^2
                      =v_C^\top G_k v_C.
\end{equation}
Each candidate gradient uses only the small selected submatrix of $G_k$.
The backend avoids copying an $r_k$-row matrix for every candidate at every
anchor. Arb encloses these quadratic forms, including subtraction error; their
printed midpoints are not used as certified values.

\begin{corollary}[Soundness with the structured backend]
Under the checked two-intercept structure and the preceding model conditions,
replacing the generic covariance and gradient calculations by the structured
Arb enclosures preserves the full-orthant certificate of Theorem~\ref{thm:global}.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:schur} and~\eqref{eq:sharedgradient} give the same exact quantities
used in the analytic bound. Enclosing each arithmetic operation gives admissible
point intervals, to which the existing partition and tail arguments apply.
\end{proof}
The two backends can have different enclosure widths. This corollary does not
guarantee identical success or runtime at a fixed precision and finite budget.

\textbf{Vertex work and memory.}
At a vertex used only for $g(v)$, the specialized code applies
\eqref{eq:schurdet} without solving all predictor right-hand sides. Large
covariance and gradient-product caches each retain at most 32 entries; the
profile cache retains at most 256. Evicted quantities are recomputed with the
same verified operations. A separate cache retains scalar determinants.
Cache eviction changes runtime and memory, not the validity of a bound.

If $d$ denotes the number of columns of $H$, the principal dense per-anchor
operations have arithmetic cost of order
$r_Lr_S^2+r_S^3+r_Lr_Sd+r_S^2d+(r_L+r_S)d^2$, followed by support-specific
small solves and quadratic forms. This is an operation-count description, not
a bit-complexity theorem or a complete-path bound: the number of anchors and
candidate supports also matters. The implementation still stores dense input
designs and predictor products. For balanced group counts the dense cubic
order in $r$ remains; the claimed improvements are measured computational savings.
The code
constructs a dense Schur complement, uses exact
rational rank reduction for the tail floor, and enumerates every pair. Bounded
large-matrix caches do not bound total process memory independently of the
witness tree or candidate count.

These are standard block-elimination, quadratic-form and caching techniques.
Sparse computational methods for mixed models are already established, including
the framework of Bates et al.~\cite{lme4}. The contribution tested here is their
use within the checked decision pipeline. \texttt{CrossedArbGaussian} explicitly
rejects weighted or multiple memberships and covariance structures with other
than two components; the original generic verifier remains available for them.

The main pilot has seven scenarios and five deterministic seeds per scenario,
with $n=400$, $p=30$, 10 origin groups and 12 destination groups. Covariance matrices
are generated by the observed crossed memberships; balanced commuting matrices
are not assumed. The base design is an intercept. Candidate columns are centered

additional start in the screening method. The comparator is an all-candidate

baseline fitting, anchor and vertex calculations, and local candidate optimization.
BLAS thread counts are one. A ``candidate fit'' is one separate multi-start
optimization for that candidate, not one likelihood evaluation. The screening
algorithm still evaluates many candidate objectives at shared ratio points.

per-method peak memory was not benchmarked.

Three datasets per configuration do not determine reliable runtime or memory
distributions. The specialization was developed after profiling smaller examples;
this is a development scaling study. It does not establish high-dimensional
statistical selection consistency, performance on thousands of groups, or
superiority to an external method. Some completed paths select zero-signal
variables despite having correct numerical certificates.

\subsection{Independent algebra and verifier checks}
Eighty combinations of a small integer-data model and variance point were
checked using exact rational reference covariance inverses and derivatives.
Reference log determinants and profile objectives were enclosed independently
at 256 bits. Both 128-bit backends contained the exact matrix/gradient values
and the independent logarithmic reference intervals. The cases cover either
ordering of group sizes, zero ratios, unused group columns and disconnected
group-incidence structures. One-entry caches forced eviction and recomputation
of previously visited points.

The structured backend also replayed all three saved stage-three large-variance
paths. A new 100-column witness was read from fresh files and independently
replayed using both the generic and structured implementations, without
optimization. Invalid data hashes, missing half-boxes and wrong winners were
rejected. Unsupported memberships and component counts raised explicit errors.
The exact tie remained unresolved, and a zero projection floor returned
\texttt{unresolved\_tail}. These checks target the new backend's algebra and
decision contract; they do not constitute a formal verification of the software.

\section{Repeated comparison of selection procedures}
\subsection{Procedures and a shared fitting engine}
The fifth stage uses five procedures. \emph{Verified BIC + pairs} is the
full-orthant method above. \emph{Local BIC + pairs} evaluates every candidate
in the same single/pair policy with separate multistart local ML fits.
\emph{Local BIC singles} removes the pair stage. \emph{ARSS-JPS policy}
uses the archived search function from the supplied article's repository.
\emph{EN-CV + ML} selects a support by cross-validated elastic net and then
fits that support as an unpenalized LMM with both random intercepts.

The unchanged archived \texttt{arss\_family} function, commit
\texttt{0cdcf976}, drives the ARSS comparison. Its source path, full revision
identifier, source hash and MIT license are retained in the bundle.
Fit and score adapters connect this search rule to
the same profile-ML engine used by the other mixed-model procedures.
The settings are $\alpha=0.01$, $K=3$, correlation gate $0.60$, and $L=5$.
Single shortlists expand until a test passes or all singles fail. Pair testing
retains the full eligible-pool denominator and both mutual conditional checks.
Scaling score algebra from $V^{-1}$ to $R^{-1}$ changes it by a common positive
factor, preserving rankings and correlations. This is a comparison of the
published search policy under a shared fit adapter; it does not reproduce the
original statsmodels engine's numerical behaviour or timings.

The local fits start on $[0,4]^2$, use four deterministic starts and a current-model
warm point for candidates, and expand active upper bounds by a factor of four,
at most six times. A fit touching the final cap is unresolved. These local fits
have no global certificate. A projected-gradient residual is recorded as a
diagnostic; it is not a proof of global optimization. The verified procedure
uses 128-bit Arb arithmetic, at most 2500 subdivisions per family and a
30-second search-iteration budget; completing an Arb check can exceed that
soft budget. A dataset worker has a separate 360-second external timeout.

The elastic-net screen uses scikit-learn~\cite{sklearnenet}. Its grid has 13
logarithmically spaced penalties from $10^{-3}$ to 1 and L1 proportions
$0.5,0.9,1$. The support threshold is $10^{-7}$ in standardized training
coordinates. The screen itself uses a marginal linear model; the crossed
covariance is introduced in the final ML refit. Accordingly, EN-CV + ML is
a specified two-stage baseline, not an implementation of penalized mixed-model
likelihood. Native shrunken elastic-net prediction errors are also saved.

\subsection{Simulation design and prediction target}
The fixed grid contains six scenarios and 50 new seeds per scenario, for
\ComparisonCases{} datasets. Every training sample has $n=300$, $p=12$ and
6 and 8 crossed groups. Group counts differ by at most one and the two
memberships are permuted independently. Standard predictors are independent
normal variables. Unless stated otherwise, random-effect standard deviations
are 0.7 and 0.8, and residual standard deviation is 1.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Signals: nonzero $(\beta_1,\beta_3,\beta_5)=(0.8,0.5,0.3)$.
\item Weak signals: $(\beta_1,\beta_3,\beta_5)=(0.35,0.25,0.15)$.
\item Suppressor: $\operatorname{Corr}(x_1,x_2)=0.995$ and
$(\beta_1,\beta_2,\beta_3)=(6,-6,0.45)$.
\item Null: all fixed coefficients zero, independent predictors.
\item Correlated null: all coefficients zero, predictor equicorrelation 0.8.
\item Group-aligned: $x_1$ is an independent origin-level normal draw plus
normal noise of standard deviation 0.1; $x_2$ is defined similarly for the
destination margin. Nonzero coefficients are $(\beta_3,\beta_5)=(0.65,0.4)$,
and random-effect standard deviations are 2 and 3.
\end{enumerate}
Training predictors are centered and standardized before constructing the
response, so the listed coefficients refer to that scale. The same training
means and standard deviations transform test predictors. The test sample has
1200 observations, 24 and 32 entirely new groups, and independent new random
effects and residual noise. Its predictor construction follows the same scenario.
All selectors receive only training arrays; test responses first enter after
every selection and coefficient fit has finished.

For each elastic-net inner split, groups on each margin are assigned to one
of three folds. At fold $k$, training excludes groups assigned to $k$ on either
margin; validation uses cells assigned to $k$ on both margins. Cells touching
one held-out margin only are omitted from that split. Thus validation groups
are absent from training on both margins, but the validation sets do not cover
all training observations. A scaling transformer is fitted inside each split.
The tuning criterion is validation mean squared error; the independent test
sample is never used to choose a penalty.

Every completed selected support receives a final local ML coefficient refit
with the two random effects. For a new test point the prediction is
$\widehat\beta_0+x_{\mathrm{new}}^\top\widehat\beta$, with no estimated random
effect for unseen groups. We report both squared error relative to the true
fixed population mean and error relative to the observed test response.
The latter includes new random effects and noise. This is marginal prediction
for new groups; it is not conditional prediction for previously observed groups.
The computational certificate covers the selected support decisions, not the
accuracy or global optimality of this final coefficient refit.

\subsection{Measurements, uncertainty and results}
A fresh subprocess handles each dataset, with one BLAS thread, independent
model objects for the five methods and a rotating execution order. End-to-end
time includes model preparation, search, tuning where applicable, all certificate
construction and verification, and the final ML refit. Additional replay,
held-out scoring, imports and disk writes are excluded. Per-method peak memory
is not inferred from this shared-process design. Wall-clock scheduling variation
is retained in the raw measurements; medians and 90th percentiles are reported.

With true support $S_0$ and selected support $\widehat S$, a dataset contributes
$|\widehat S\cap S_0|/|S_0|$ to TPR when $S_0\ne\varnothing$, and
$|\widehat S\setminus S_0|/\max(1,|\widehat S|)$ to false-discovery proportion.
Their Monte Carlo means estimate TPR and FDR for the stated scenarios.
We also record exact support recovery, any-false-selection events and recovery
of both suppressor variables, irrespective of whether they entered together.
For an independent-dataset metric $m_b$, the Monte Carlo standard error is
$s_m/\sqrt B$. Event proportions receive 95\% Wilson intervals. Paired
differences use the same datasets; observations within a test set are not
treated as independent Monte Carlo replicates.

The verified method completed \ComparisonComplete{}/\ComparisonCases{} paths,
with \ComparisonSteps{} verified decisions and \ComparisonPairs{} pair additions.
All completed verified paths were replayed successfully. The full decision
sequence agreed with local BIC + pairs in \ComparisonAgreed{} cases; the raw
summary records the comparison denominator and all method failures separately.
No partial path is counted as completed selection. Quality summaries use
completed runs, with explicit denominators; paired summaries use joint completion.
An observed local agreement does not replace the independent interval witness.

\subsection{Interpretation and remaining scope}
\input{results/comparison/comparison_interpretation.tex}
The BIC single/pair procedure and ARSS-JPS use different stopping rules, while
elastic net is tuned for prediction. At $n=300$, a BIC single addition is
compared against $\log(300)$; ARSS uses a chi-squared test at 0.01.
These procedures therefore have no matched nominal familywise-error level.
One method's increased recovery must be assessed alongside false selections
and predictive error. Certified computation does not make BIC a procedure with
proved statistical error control.

The median paired time of local BIC + pairs divided by verified BIC + pairs
is \ComparisonLocalRatio{}. This describes the complete implementations:
their local starting and domain-expansion policies differ from the checked
tail construction. It is not an isolated estimate of the cost of Arb or of
one bound formula. The previous matched backend study remains the appropriate
comparison when only the verified linear-algebra implementation changes.

Fifty replicates per scenario yield appreciable Monte Carlo uncertainty. For
example, zero events in 50 trials still gives a Wilson upper bound near 7.1\%.
The present study fixes $p=12$ and 14 random-effect columns and does not combine
statistical replication with the largest dimensions from stage four.
Six separate technical development cases were excluded from this grid.
The recorded grid and thresholds were frozen before its first run; this is
not a preregistered study or an external replication of the research team.

The recent \texttt{glmmsel} implementation~\cite{glmmselmanual} accepts one
cluster-membership vector and jointly selects fixed and random slopes.
It was inspected but not run: directly substituting one of our crossed
groupings would change the covariance model. No performance claim about that
package is inferred from these tables. A dedicated comparison on a common
supported model class and an application with complete training-fold selection
remain necessary.

\section{Novelty audit and route to a CSDA manuscript}
The focused audit is recorded in \texttt{NOVELTY\_AUDIT\_PL.md}, including searches,
access limitations and the exact scope of each comparison. Profiling and compact
shape optimization have direct precedents in de Carvalho et al.~\cite{carvalho};
the projection floor is closely related to Zhou et al.~\cite{zhou}. Safe
screening for $\ell_0$ regression is already developed by Atamt\"urk and
G\'omez~\cite{atamturk}. Generic deterministic global optimization with lower
bounds is established, including $\alpha$BB~\cite{alphabb}. None of these broad
ideas should be advertised as a first discovery here.

The most relevant modern comparators include Thompson et al.~\cite{thompson}
and the relaxation-based mixed-model selection of Sholokhov et al.~\cite{sholokhov}.
Ghosh and Thoresen~\cite{ghosh} consider robust probabilistic sure screening;
the current audit accessed their abstract, not the full technical PDF. For many
crossed groups, the GLS backfitting work of Ghosh, Hastie and Owen~\cite{backfit}
is a relevant computational reference. It does not itself provide the present
family-level ML decision certificate.

The defensible prospective contribution is the specialized combination of
candidate-family bounds under covariance re-estimation, shared computation,
verified stopping, and replayable single/pair path witnesses, now including
explicitly checked unbounded-tail exclusion under a positive-floor condition. No identical
complete construction was identified in the inspected texts. This is a bounded
literature finding, not a proof of historical priority; parts of the broader
literature and one recent full text remain unchecked. The standalone convexity
lemma and generic $O(d_B^2)$ bound consistency are not sufficient novelty claims.

Before submission, the main remaining scientific work is:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Tighten the conservative tail cover and investigate other sufficient
conditions for models whose projection floor is zero. Retain unresolved outcomes
when an implemented certificate's conditions cannot be verified.
\item Extend beyond the measured two-intercept specialization: develop sparse
covariance algebra and a pair strategy for many more groups and candidates,
including simultaneous growth. Separate the benefits of individual changes
if making component-specific performance claims.
\item If extending certification to ARSS-JPS, check its actual likelihood-ratio
decisions and stopping rules. The archived policy is already used as a comparator;
the current path certificate applies to the separate BIC policy.
\item Extend the shared-engine ARSS comparison to native contemporary
penalized/$\ell_0$ implementations on model classes each supports. Different
statistical objectives require separate computational and predictive comparisons.
\item Broaden the repeated study to larger dimensions and more replications
for rare error events, and analyze at least two real datasets
with selection inside training folds. Justify the selected penalty or inferential
calibration for the intended crossed-effects regime.
\end{enumerate}
The completed fourth stage adds a checked structural specialization and matched
whole-path time/memory evidence to the full-orthant guarantees. The fifth stage
adds repeated selection and new-group prediction comparisons with explicit
uncertainty and a source-checked adaptation of the published ARSS search rule.
It provides a concrete methodological core; broader validation remains necessary.
